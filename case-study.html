The brief

Five tiers, three thousand members, one renewal date.

IEB coaches home inspectors on running their businesses. Five membership tiers from $197 to $1,997 a month, sold through Kajabi, with everything else living in Zoho One: member records, lead pipelines, onboarding, retention, sponsors.

The renewal date made it a hard cutover rather than a gradual one. Kajabi stays as the course platform. Everything downstream of it, the CRM, the automation, the custom data model, and the audits that prove none of it silently broke, is the work.

  • No dropped signups during the move, on any of the five tiers
  • No missed onboarding call while the kickoff booking flow changed platforms
  • No lost sponsor record across a partner programme that was half in a spreadsheet
  • Everything archived before anything in the old system was cancelled

The part nobody scopes for is what you find on the way in. Most of what follows was not on the brief. It was found, proven and fixed because a migration forces you to read every record.

How it shipped

Three phases, sequenced so nothing live ever waited on something unbuilt.

Kajabi checkout→Zapier→GoHighLevel→Zoho One

That was the starting shape. By July the sale itself moved into GoHighLevel and the integration ran the other way, granting course access back to Kajabi. That one change is why membership data is now born clean at the point of sale instead of arriving secondhand.

Case files

The problems that actually mattered.

Four of the first five are problems the client did not know existed. Anyone can point and click a CRM into shape. Catching the thing quietly eating data on the way through is the part that protects a business.

Data integrity
Resolved 7 July 2026 · audit log, cloned draft, one-by-one verification

A workflow that succeeded every time, and erased three fields every time it did.

The situation

New members arrived with blank Monthly Payment, Annual Payment and Membership Date. The integration wrote them correctly at checkout, and thirty seconds later they were gone. Records looked half-filled rather than broken, which is why it survived.

Why it was hard

The culprit was a legitimate workflow stamping Role on a new member. Its update action carried three extra rows with no value. In GoHighLevel an empty update row does not mean leave it alone, it means write blank. The run history looked green every time.

Outcome

Cloned to a draft first for rollback, three rows deleted, the legitimate action untouched. Zero records wiped after the fix, proven across 677 members and 2,740 contacts. Seven records still showed a blank; each was inspected, and the five real ones were routed to a separate backfill rather than folded into the win.

Data recovery
Resolved 1 August 2026 · full legacy export, content matching, staged resumable posting

Ninety-five percent of five years of note history was missing, and nobody knew.

The situation

The client passed on a low-priority remark: the salesperson said a lot of his notes had not migrated. He was right, and it was worse than he knew. Zoho held 45,875 notes. GHL held 2,646, from an undocumented partial import.

What was done

Every note pulled and archived, making that file the only surviving copy. All 3,850 contacts matched by content. Routing by contact type, not by whoever typed the note, because authorship in the old system is history, not ownership.

Outcome

27,627 notes posted across 2,738 contacts. All 9,337 of the salesperson's, and 18,290 for operations. Verified live in the interface and by re-pulling from the API twice. Delivered two days after a request the client had marked low priority.

Judgement
Resolved 16 June 2026 · tiered match logic, one master file

The duplicate flag that would have deleted a sixth of the lead list.

The situation

An automated dedup flagged 1,662 of 4,130 leads. 1,597 of them were flagged only because they share a phone number with another lead. In home inspection that is almost always coworkers on one office line.

The principle

A duplicate flag is a question, not an instruction. Exact email is decisive, phone is a strong signal but unreliable for people, name plus company is strong, name alone is review only. The cost of a wrong delete and a wrong keep are not the same size.

Outcome

About 50 leads removed before import. All 1,597 shared-phone leads kept. Nothing was deleted.

Architecture
Shipped 16 to 22 July 2026 · five checkouts, reverse webhooks, old path kept as rollback

Moving checkout to remove the root cause instead of patching the symptom.

The situation

Every sync problem traced to one fact: the sale started in Kajabi, so the CRM got membership date, payment and tier secondhand. The vendor's own fix carried an August estimate.

What was done

Custom checkout pages for every tier matching the brand members already knew. Each fires a per-tier workflow that stamps tags, role, status, level, date and payment. A reverse webhook hands the course platform its grant. No card fields on the page, so no PCI liability.

Outcome

Three tiers published by 16 July, all five cleared for live testing by the 22nd, three weeks ahead of the vendor's estimate. Existing subscribers kept billing where they were with no change.

What went wrong
8 July 2026, reported the same week · the rule that came out of it

Thirty emails I did not mean to send.

What happened

During a routine tag cleanup on exited members, a published workflow enrolled 30 contacts and sent 30 "you have exited" emails to people who left long ago. Its trigger was Contact Tag with no tag named, which fires on any tag event, removal included. I had planned the write off a snapshot from an earlier session.

What I did

Reported it in that week's review under its own heading before anyone asked. Then wrote the rule: before any bulk tag write, re-pull the account in the same session, open every published workflow that could watch that tag, and unpublish it for the duration.

Outcome

That rule is why the exit import of 747 contacts two weeks later ran with zero unintended sends, and why a later guard pass across 976 contacts sent nothing. The same failure has not recurred.

Platform defect, proven
11 to 17 September 2026 · affiliate manager, order timestamps, the platform's own price data

Tracking installed, every endpoint answering, and no commission ever created.

The situation

The affiliate programme recorded nothing. The obvious reads were "the script is not installed" and "the cookie domain is wrong". Both were true once, both were fixed, and commissions still never appeared.

What was done

Proved the loader on every checkout, the cookie on the right domain, every affiliate endpoint returning 201, and the platform's own checkout recognising the session. Then a test purchase through a real affiliate link with the order timestamp, and the platform's price data showing annual variants can never earn commission at all.

Outcome

Diagnosed to the platform's own conversion pipeline, packaged for the vendor's developers with a recorded walkthrough. Until they fix it the client has a manual ledger, and knows exactly which products it covers.

Ops tooling
27 September 2026 · one read-only script, fifteen CSVs, run weekly

The audit that finds the problem before the person does.

The situation

Every request from the operations coordinator had the same shape. She touches one member, sees a field that looks wrong, and reports it after the fact. Blank teammate fields, an exit that never fired, a renamed contact, texts not arriving, a wrong reimbursement amount.

What was done

One script measures every one of those shapes account-wide in a single run: fill gaps, parent-child drift, exits without the guard tag, subscriptions whose email differs from the contact, payment saved as the discounted first charge, nameless duplicates, customers still worked as leads, unreachable recipients.

Outcome

Its second run, two days after the first, caught two paying members whose contact records had been deleted in a cleanup pass, before either of them missed a reminder. That is the job: shipping a fix is easy, proving it stayed fixed is the work.

Working around the platform

Half of a good GoHighLevel build is knowing what it cannot do.

When the platform has no logic for something, the choice is to tell the client no, or to build the missing piece outside it and wire it back in. Twenty Zapier integrations exist on this account because of the second choice.

  • No "record was linked" trigger so a Role change is the door into a webhook that fills the teammate's fields from the parent record, blanks only
  • No scheduler so a weekly Zap re-sweeps partner links and a monthly one stamps tenure and purges stale tags
  • No dropdown writes from the native custom-object action it reports success and writes nothing, so a webhook to the v2 API does it with a read-back
  • No duplicate-card bypass the rule ignores card status, so a baseline snapshot was taken and the account setting flipped with a diff afterwards
  • No trigger on integration writes Contact Changed does not fire for the course platform's writes, tag triggers do, and the exit path was rebuilt around that

The code

Python for the bulk work. All of it against the live API.

Bulk work does not belong in a workflow builder. Anything that touches thousands of records was written in Python, run from a terminal against the GoHighLevel and Zoho APIs, and built to the same pattern every time.

plan

Read-only

Writes a snapshot to disk and says what would change. Nothing else.

canary

Three records

Proves the write is a partial update, diffs every field, and stops for a human.

go

Read back every write

Confirms each one before the next. Three failures in a row aborts. A short pull prints nothing.

Then verify re-reads everything against the plan and restore puts it all back from the snapshot. Every run leaves a ledger, so a job that dies at record 900 of 4,000 resumes instead of double-writing.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
ieb_state_snapshot.py - the live-state snapshot + diff one-paste.

WHY THIS EXISTS
Every "how many are missing" question so far has been answered
against a different snapshot, so the numbers never line up twice.
This writes ONE timestamped state file per run, always the same
shape, so any later run can be diffed against it.
"""

Notes 56,783 · Activities 44,468 · Calls 20,487 · Events 6,387 · Leads 4,483 · Campaigns 2,142 · Contacts 1,985 · Accounts 1,447 · six custom modules. 179 MB.
