Onboarding automation for memberships

What happens in the first 45 days after someone pays?

Before

Checkout lived on the course platform, members were added to the CRM by hand, and the ops team chased surveys, kickoff calls and portal access manually.

Built

Four tier checkouts in the CRM that write the member record, grant course access through a reverse webhook, start a welcome and survey sequence, book and remind for the kickoff call, assign the ops team's tasks, and hand off to 300 days of retention touches.

The part that takes experience

Rejoiners are handled at checkout (old exit tags stripped, cancellation date cleared) so a returning member does not get a goodbye email. Retention is driven off a custom date so history could be backfilled and every existing member enrolled on day one.

KPI dashboards with history

Can your growth team see last year next to this month?

Before

The CRM could only chart from its own start date. Eighteen months of call, lead and consultation history sat in the old system.

Built

A lead history object and a monthly rollup object loaded from the old CRM's exports, five dashboard widgets on top, and the forward feed wired to the same definitions so the chart has no seam at go-live.

The part that takes experience

A custom date field accepts any date, so history was backfilled record by record. The rollup object only exists for ratios the chart tool cannot compute. Answer rate uses a 30 second cutoff agreed with the client, not the platform default.

Partner program operations

How many partner deadlines does your team chase by email?

Before

Sponsors, podcast guests and magazine submissions tracked across a sheet, a legacy CRM and inboxes. Reminders sent by hand every quarter.

Built

A partner company record with fourteen mirrored fields, a sponsorship purchase flow that creates the pipeline card and notifies the team, podcast booking and publish notices, and quarterly submission reminders that stop themselves when the partner submits.

The part that takes experience

The reminder audience is a tag, the stop condition is a tag, and the quarter's dates live in three custom values. Changing the quarter is three fields, not a rebuild.

Debugging

Can you debug the platform itself, or only the workflows on it?

Found a HighLevel bug and got it fixed

An RSS email campaign was rendering a block tag wrong on every send. I isolated it, wrote the reproduction with the raw feed and the rendered output, filed it, and when HighLevel shipped the fix I proved it on the next live send before telling the client it was done.

Support said one thing, the API said another

Support called a wrong SMS sender stamp a display quirk. I pulled the stored message records: three contacts with one owner showed five different senders across 82 messages. The stamp follows the sending number, and I had the data to show it.

714 skipped emails, one real cause

Support said the skips were already-sent contacts. That explained 152 of 714. The platform's own skipped export showed 351 unmailable contacts as the real cause.

"Our emails are going to spam"

They were not. Same domain, same sender, both months. The statistics dashboard showed 0.78% bounce and 0.018% complaints. The only change was list size. The fix was list hygiene, not a new domain.

How I work

Why does nothing break quietly?

Read the record before flagging it

A list view is not evidence. I open the record, check who created it and when, then speak.

Preview every write

Plan, go, verify, restore. Nothing bulk runs without a dry run and an archive.

Tag every claim

Measured, documented or inferred. The third kind never reaches a client.

Only fix what I built

Anything else gets reported with the fix attached. You stay in control of your account.

Keep a rulings file

Decisions are dated and kept, so closed questions stay closed.

Audit from the system, not screenshots

Dump, render, check, diff. Weekly.

The objection

“We tried Zapier and it broke.”

Zaps break when they trust their input. Mine check the source record first, cap their writes and log a report. The sync Zaps here ran 178 times in 90 days with one error, fixed the same week.
